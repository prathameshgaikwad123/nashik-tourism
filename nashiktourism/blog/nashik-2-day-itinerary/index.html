Nashik 2 Day Itinerary
Temples, Ghats & Wine in 48 Hours

A suggested two days in Nashik — sacred ghats at dawn, ancient caves, vineyards and local food. Complete day-by-day guide.

Travel TipsApril 20268 min read

How current is this guide?

Seasons, prices and arrangements change. Details in this guide have not been verified against official sources. Check before you travel. See our sources.

Nashik rewards travellers who stay at least two nights. One day is enough to touch the surface. Two days lets you experience the city: the ghat before dawn, ancient Buddhist caves, vineyards in afternoon light, and a proper evening. This itinerary can work year-round and also suits Kumbh Mela 2027 visitors wanting to see Nashik beyond just the ghat.

A note on times and prices: the clock times in the headings below are suggestions for pacing your day, not venue opening hours. This site has not verified opening hours, entry fees, prices or aarti times, so check each place locally. Durations are approximate.

Day 1 — The Sacred City

5:30 AM — Ramkund Ghat at Dawn

Wake before sunrise. Walk to Ramkund Ghat. Morning light on the Godavari, bells, the first bathers on stone steps — this is Nashik at its most authentic. The morning aarti, where held (times vary; check locally), can be among the most atmospheric experiences in the city. Take a holy dip if you wish. Allow approximately 45 minutes here.

7:00 AM — Breakfast in the Panchavati Lanes

Eat at one of the old dhabas behind Ramkund. Order sabudana khichdi, poha with shev, or Nashik misal pav. Prices vary. A common tip is to look for places where locals are eating.

8:00 AM — Kalaram Temple

Near Ramkund. The temple is known for its black Ram idol. It is architecturally notable and is historically associated with Dr. Ambedkar's temple-entry satyagraha. Allow approximately 30–45 minutes.

9:00 AM — Sita Gufa and Panchavati Banyan Tree

A short walk from Kalaram. Sita Gufa is the cave where Sita is believed to have stayed. Small, atmospheric, actively worshipped. The Panchavati banyan grove, traditionally linked to the Ramayana, is nearby. Allow approximately 20 minutes.

10:00 AM — Pandavleni Caves

Take an auto to Pandavleni Caves (about 5 km, not verified; agree the fare first). Ancient Buddhist rock-cut caves carved into Trirashmi hill. Extraordinary workmanship — Buddha carvings, stupas, monks' cells. Around 200 steps to climb (not verified). The views of Nashik from the top are a highlight for many visitors. Allow approximately 1–1.5 hours.

12:30 PM — Lunch

Look for a sit-down restaurant serving a Maharashtrian thali. Earlier editions of this guide named Satkar Restaurant and Kapila Restaurant, which we have not re-verified. Prices vary.

2:30 PM — Muktidham Temple

A white marble temple featuring replicas of the 12 Jyotirlingas under one roof. Not ancient but architecturally striking. Good for those wanting to symbolically visit all Jyotirlingas. Allow approximately 30 minutes.

7:00 PM — Evening Aarti at Ramkund

Return for the evening aarti, where held (times vary; check locally) — diyas floated on the river, priests chanting, flames on dark water. It can be deeply moving. Walk the riverside lanes, buy puja items, have chai. Dinner at a Panchavati restaurant — Marathi thali or misal pav.

Day 2 — Wine, Nature and Trimbakeshwar

6:00 AM — Trimbakeshwar Temple (Early Visit)

Take a shared jeep or cab to Trimbakeshwar (about 28 km; the journey is commonly estimated at 45–60 minutes; both figures not verified). Going early may mean shorter queues, but temple timings and queue lengths vary and have not been verified here. The Trimbakeshwar Shivalinga is traditionally described as distinctive among the 12 Jyotirlingas, with three faces said to represent Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva. Early morning darshan is often described as calm. Explore Kushavarta Kund, reported as the Trimbakeshwar bathing site for the Shaiva akharas, after the temple.

10:00 AM — Return to Nashik

Allow time for the return journey. Store luggage if checking out. Freshen up.

11:30 AM — Sula Vineyards

Take a cab to Sula (about 15 km, not verified). Allow approximately 2–3 hours: vineyard walk, a wine tasting session if you wish (ask the estate about booking, especially on weekends), and lunch at one of the estate's dining options. Check the minimum legal drinking age and carry ID if you plan to taste wine. This is the cultural counterpoint to Day 1's spiritual intensity — and that contrast is a big part of Nashik's character. If time permits, other wineries in the area may be worth a look; check which are open to visitors.

3:00 PM — Gangapur Dam

A scenic reservoir in the Sahyadri foothills, reached from Nashik; check the route, access and any restrictions before you go. Good for a quiet walk and views, and it may suit a sunset if you're staying another night. Take care near the water.

5:00 PM — Shopping and Departure

Markets such as the MG Road area are commonly mentioned for religious items in copper and brass, Maharashtrian textiles and local sweets (try chirote and shankarpali). If you plan to take wine home, check the rules on carrying alcohol first.

Practical Notes

  • Day 1 transport: Consider hiring an auto for the morning circuit (Pandavleni and several stops); fares vary, so agree the fare and duration before you start
  • Sula booking: Ask the estate about booking a tasting in advance, especially on weekends
  • Trimbakeshwar queue: Queues vary by day and season and are often longer at weekends and on festival days. Temple timings and any darshan arrangements have not been verified here; check with the temple
  • Kumbh Mela 2027 adjustment: Access, road and transport arrangements on Amrit Snan days have not been announced and may differ from normal days. Treat the Day 1 morning as flexible and build in extra time; see our Kumbh dates and latest updates
  • Where to stay: Panchavati area for Day 1 sites. Gangapur Road for Sula proximity

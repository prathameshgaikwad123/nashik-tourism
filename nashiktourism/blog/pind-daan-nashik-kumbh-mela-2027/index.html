Pind Daan at Nashik Kumbh Mela 2027
Guide — Rituals & Priests

A guide to ancestral rites at Ramkund — what Pind Daan is, how it is traditionally performed and how to find a priest at Nashik. Fees vary, so agree any fee beforehand.

Nashik Kumbh Mela 2027 is not only about bathing in the Godavari. For many families, visiting Nashik during the Simhastha period is specifically for performing Pind Daan — the sacred ancestral rite believed to grant liberation (moksha) to departed souls. Nashik is traditionally regarded as an important site for Pind Daan, and many devotees believe the Simhastha period adds to the spiritual merit of the rite. This guide explains what Pind Daan is, how it is traditionally performed, where it is done and how to find a priest. We have not verified ritual details with priests, so treat this as general background and confirm with your own priest.

🕛 What Is Pind Daan?

Pind Daan (also called Shraddha or Pitru Tarpan) is a Hindu ritual of offering rice balls (pindas), sesame seeds, water and prayers to departed ancestors. It is believed to help the soul of the deceased attain peace and liberation from the cycle of rebirth. Devotees traditionally regard Nashik on the Godavari as a sacred place for Pind Daan.

Why Nashik Is Special for Pind Daan

The Godavari river at Nashik is traditionally regarded as significant for ancestral rites. The Ramkund ghat in Panchavati is where Lord Rama himself is believed to have performed the last rites for his father King Dasharatha during his exile. This association is why many devotees, particularly Vaishnava families, regard Ramkund as a significant place for Pind Daan.

Many devotees believe that performing Pind Daan at Nashik during the Simhastha Kumbh Mela carries added spiritual weight, because of the combination of the Kumbh period, the Godavari river and the planetary alignment associated with Simhastha. This is a matter of faith and tradition.

Where to Perform Pind Daan in Nashik

Ramkund Ghat (Primary Site)

The ghat traditionally associated with Pind Daan in Nashik is Ramkund itself — the same ghat where pilgrims bathe during Kumbh Mela. Pandas (hereditary priests who specialise in ancestral rites) have traditionally conducted the ceremonies there. What arrangements, designated spaces or restrictions will apply during the Kumbh Mela has not been announced in sources we could locate.

Asthi Visarjan

Asthi Visarjan (immersion of ashes of the deceased) is traditionally performed in the Godavari at Nashik. Some families bring the ashes of a recently deceased relative to Nashik for this purpose, often followed by Pind Daan. The Godavari is believed to carry the soul toward liberation when ashes are immersed in it. We have not confirmed which area is designated for Asthi Visarjan or what arrangements will apply during the Kumbh Mela.

Trimbakeshwar (For Narayan Nagbali and Tripindi Shraddha)

Trimbakeshwar near Nashik is traditionally associated with two complex ancestral rituals: Narayan Nagbali (traditionally performed to free a soul said to be troubled by a serpent curse) and Tripindi Shraddha (traditionally for ancestors who did not receive proper last rites). These are described as multi-day rituals conducted by priests at Trimbakeshwar. They are separate from regular Pind Daan at Ramkund. Please confirm the details, and which priests or organisations to approach, locally.

How Pind Daan Is Performed — Step by Step

The sequence below is a general description. Practice varies between priests and families, so follow your priest's guidance:

  • Day before: Arrive in Nashik. Contact a panda (ghat priest) through your hotel or directly at the ghat. Discuss your family's gotra (lineage), the names of the deceased and the nature of the ritual needed.
  • Morning of the ritual: Wake early (traditionally before sunrise). Bathing in the Godavari at Ramkund first is traditionally done to purify the performer before the ritual.
  • Ritual preparation: The panda prepares the ritual items — rice balls (pindas), sesame (til), water, flowers, tulsi leaves, ghee and darba grass. You may be asked to provide the gotra and the names of the departed.
  • Ritual performance: The panda guides you through mantras and offerings. You offer pindas to the river while invoking the names of your departed ancestors. How long it takes depends on the ritual and the priest.
  • Tarpan: Water is offered to ancestors with sesame seeds — called Pitru Tarpan.
  • Dana: After the ritual, offerings or donations are customarily made to the priest, symbolising giving in the name of the ancestor. Agree any amount with the priest beforehand.

Finding a Reliable Priest (Panda) at Nashik

Many families are unsure how to choose a priest. As anywhere popular with pilgrims, it is sensible to be cautious about inflated fees or unnecessary add-ons. Some practical suggestions:

  • Ask your hotel or host: They may be able to suggest a priest they know who conducts shraddha rites at Ramkund. Treat any suggestion as a starting point, not an endorsement.
  • Check credentials locally: Priests and priest organisations operate at Ramkund. We have not verified any of them, so ask other families for references and check credentials locally before you commit.
  • Agree on the fee beforehand: Fees vary with the ritual's length and complexity, and we have no verified figures. Agree the total amount, and what it includes, before starting. Do not agree to additions mid-ritual.
  • Be cautious of touts: Be wary of anyone who approaches you aggressively near the ghat steps claiming to be a priest or guide. Take your time and choose a priest on your own terms.

Cost of Pind Daan at Nashik Kumbh Mela 2027

  • Priest fees at Ramkund: These vary and we have no verified figures. Agree the amount with the priest before the ritual begins
  • Ritual materials (puja samagri): Ask your priest what is needed and whether they provide it; compare prices if buying from shops near the ghat
  • Dana / offerings: As per your capacity — there is no fixed amount
  • Narayan Nagbali and Tripindi Shraddha at Trimbakeshwar: We have no verified fees. Ask the priests at Trimbakeshwar directly and agree the cost in advance
⚠️ Important

Demand for priests may be high on busy days during the Kumbh Mela, although attendance estimates vary widely and none is confirmed. Consider contacting a priest before your trip if you have a specific date in mind, through your accommodation or a trusted contact.

Most Auspicious Dates for Pind Daan During Kumbh 2027

Which days are considered auspicious for ancestral rites depends on tradition and on your family priest. Confirm dates against a panchang or with your priest. Days often mentioned include:

  • Sarva Pitru Amavasya — the concluding day of Pitru Paksha, traditionally regarded as an important day for ancestral rites (date to be confirmed with a panchang)
  • Amrit Snan dates — reported as August 2, August 31, September 11–12, 2027 (not yet confirmed); expect very large crowds
  • Any Amavasya (new moon) during the Kumbh period
  • Guru Purnima
  • Pitru Paksha — the annual fortnight traditionally dedicated to ancestors (dates per the panchang)

Frequently Asked Questions

Can any Hindu perform Pind Daan at Nashik, or only certain communities?

Practice differs between priests and traditions, and we cannot speak for them. Please ask the priest you plan to engage whether they can conduct the ritual for your family's tradition.

Do I need to bring ashes to perform Pind Daan?

Generally not. Pind Daan is traditionally performed for ancestors whether or not ashes are brought, and the priest will usually ask for the names and gotra of the deceased. Asthi Visarjan (immersion of ashes) is a separate rite for the recently deceased. Confirm with your priest.

How long does Pind Daan take at Nashik?

This depends on the ritual and the priest, and we have no verified figure. Ask your priest in advance. The Trimbakeshwar rituals are described as multi-day, so ask the priests there about the time needed.

Can women perform Pind Daan?

Traditions differ. The rite has traditionally been performed by the eldest son, but practice varies, and some families have daughters or widows perform it. Ask your priest whether they will conduct the ritual in your circumstances.

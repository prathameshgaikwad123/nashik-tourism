Ramkund Ghat Nashik
Complete Guide — History, Visiting Tips & Kumbh 2027

The sacred bathing ghat on the Godavari river. Heart of Nashik spirituality and Kumbh Mela 2027 site.

Nashik GuidesApril 20267 min read

Ramkund is one of the most sacred bathing ghats in Nashik and, according to reports, a main bathing site for the Kumbh Mela 2027. Located in the Panchavati area on the banks of the Godavari river, it draws pilgrims and visitors throughout the year. Attendance estimates for the Kumbh Mela vary widely and none is confirmed. This is where Lord Rama is believed to have bathed during his 14-year exile, which is why the water here is deeply revered by many devotees.

History and Significance of Ramkund

Ramkund is not merely a bathing ghat — it is one of the most spiritually charged sites in the Hindu tradition. According to the Valmiki Ramayana, Lord Rama along with Sita and Lakshmana settled in the Panchavati forest near Nashik during their 14 years of Vanvas (exile). Ramkund is traditionally believed to be the spot where Lord Rama bathed in the Godavari. The word "Kund" means a sacred water body, and "Ram" refers to Lord Rama himself.

The ghat’s stone steps leading down to the river are commonly said to date from the Peshwa era, though this site has not verified the dates of its construction. Ramkund has been a place of pilgrimage for many generations, and it is one of the sites of the Kumbh Mela, which returns to Nashik in a roughly 12-year cycle.

Religiously, bathing at Ramkund is believed to wash away sins (papa), help ancestors attain mukti (liberation), and bring spiritual blessings. This is why the ghat is also used for the immersion of ashes (asthi visarjan) — the last rites for the deceased — as it is believed the Godavari carries the soul to liberation.

🌟 Best Time to Visit Ramkund

Many visitors find the morning aarti the most spiritually charged and visually striking time to visit; check locally for the aarti time. The evening aarti with diyas on the river is also beautiful and is often less crowded than the morning. Weekend mornings tend to be the most crowded non-Kumbh hours.

How to Reach Ramkund Ghat

From Nashik Road Railway Station

Nashik Road Railway Station is about 8 km from Ramkund (distance not verified). Auto-rickshaws and taxis are the usual way to cover it; fares and journey times vary, so agree the fare before you set off. Road and parking arrangements near the ghat during the Kumbh Mela have not been announced; expect restrictions and some walking on busy days. See parking and roads and our transport page.

From Nashik City Centre (CBS Bus Stand)

Ramkund is in the Panchavati area. Auto-rickshaws run between the CBS and Panchavati; fares and journey times vary, so agree the fare first. A separate Panchavati bus stand is said to be nearer Ramkund (not verified), and city buses may serve the area; check locally.

From Trimbakeshwar

Trimbakeshwar is roughly 28 km away, with a journey commonly estimated at 45–60 minutes depending on traffic (both figures not verified). Shared jeeps and private taxis are commonly used; fares vary, so agree the fare before you go.

By Walking (from Panchavati)

If you are staying in the Panchavati area, Ramkund is likely within walking distance, depending on your hotel. Ask your hotel for the exact walking route. The lanes near Ramkund are narrow and best navigated on foot.

Rules at Ramkund Ghat

The points below are general etiquette, not official regulations. This site cannot confirm which rules local authorities or temple trusts enforce, so follow any signs and instructions you see on site.

  • Photography: Usually fine in open areas, but do not photograph other bathers without permission. Signs on site take precedence.
  • Clothing: Dress modestly. Whether changing facilities are available nearby has not been verified, so do not rely on them.
  • Footwear: Footwear is commonly removed before going down the ghat steps; follow signs and what others do.
  • Plastic: Please avoid single-use plastic near the river and carry your litter out.
  • Food: Please be considerate about eating on the ghat steps, which are used for worship.
  • Loud music / speakers: Please keep music and speakers off; the ghat is a place of worship.
  • Immersion of idols: Any rules can change around festivals; check with the local authorities.
  • Asthi visarjan (ash immersion): Families usually arrange the rites with local priests (pandas); ask locally about current requirements.

What to See Near Ramkund

Ramkund sits in the heart of the Panchavati area, with several temples and sacred sites nearby. Distances and walking times have not been verified, so ask locally:

  • Kalaram Temple — Known for its black Ram idol, one of Nashik's best-known temples
  • Sita Gufa — The cave where Sita is believed to have stayed during Vanvas
  • Panchavati Banyan Tree — The banyan grove traditionally linked to the Ramayana's Panchavati
  • Ganga Ghat — Another ghat on the Godavari, near Ramkund
  • Muktidham Temple — White marble temple featuring replicas of India's 12 Jyotirlingas
  • Coin Museum — A museum devoted to coins; check its location and opening hours before planning a visit

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Ramkund open every day?

Ramkund is an open riverside ghat and visitors come throughout the year. Hours of approximately 5:30 AM to 9:30 PM have been quoted, but they are not verified, and access can change on festival days or in bad weather. Check locally.

What is the best time to avoid crowds at Ramkund?

Weekday afternoons are generally quieter, and weekday mornings are usually less crowded than weekends. Weekend mornings tend to be the busiest non-festival times. This site does not measure crowds, and they change with festivals and the season.

Can non-Hindus visit Ramkund?

Ramkund is an open public ghat, and visitors of all backgrounds, including international visitors, generally come to see it. This site has not verified any rule about who may bathe or take part in rites, so ask locally and follow signs and the priests’ guidance.

Is there parking near Ramkund?

Parking in the Panchavati area can be limited, and arrangements have not been verified. Parking and road arrangements for the Kumbh Mela have not been announced; expect vehicle restrictions and some walking on busy days. See parking and roads.

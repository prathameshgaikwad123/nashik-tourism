Trimbakeshwar Temple

Trimbakeshwar is a temple town in the hills west of Nashik, built around one of the twelve Jyotirlingas. It stands near the source of the Godavari, and during the Simhastha Kumbh Mela it is one of the two centres of the bathing rituals — the other being the ghats in Nashik city itself.

Why visit Trimbakeshwar Temple

Trimbakeshwar is the reason a great many people come to Nashik district at all. The temple draws pilgrims through the year, and the town around it has grown up entirely in service of that visit — lodgings, priests, ritual supplies, queues.

It also feels quite different from Nashik city. The road climbs out of the plain into hills that stay green long after the monsoon, and the town is compact enough to walk. If you are making the trip from Nashik, it is worth allowing more time than the distance alone suggests.

Things to see and do

The temple itself is the centre of any visit, and most people combine it with the tank at Kushavarta nearby, which is the bathing point associated with the Kumbh Mela at Trimbakeshwar. The surrounding town is small and easily explored on foot between visits.

History and significance

Trimbakeshwar is counted among the twelve Jyotirlingas, the shrines to Shiva that hold particular importance in Hindu pilgrimage. Its position near the source of the Godavari ties it directly to the river that shapes the rest of Nashik — and to the Kumbh Mela, which follows that river down to the ghats at Ramkund.

During the Kumbh Mela

Kushavarta Tirtha, beside the temple, is the Mela bathing point at Trimbakeshwar (Shaiva akharas).

Access, queues and crowd rules during the Mela will be set by the Authority and the police and may differ from ordinary days. We could not find a published plan for this site, so we do not describe one. See the Kumbh hub and live updates.

Best time to visit

Nashik's year divides fairly clearly into the monsoon, the cool months that follow it, and a hot stretch before the rains return. Our month-by-month guide to Nashik covers how that plays out across the district.

Travel tips

  • Temples in Maharashtra generally expect modest dress. It is worth checking current arrangements before you travel, particularly around festival dates.
  • Queues lengthen considerably on auspicious days. If your dates are flexible, an ordinary weekday will be a very different experience from a festival one.
  • Trimbakeshwar and Nashik city are separate destinations. Plan them as two trips rather than assuming you can fit both comfortably into a few hours.

Frequently asked questions

Where is Trimbakeshwar?

Trimbakeshwar is a town in Nashik district, Maharashtra, in the hills to the west of Nashik city. Our route guide covers travel between the two.

Is Trimbakeshwar part of the Kumbh Mela?

Yes. The Simhastha Kumbh Mela at Nashik centres on two locations: the ghats at Ramkund in Nashik city, and Kushavarta at Trimbakeshwar. Our Kumbh Mela 2027 guide explains how the two connect.

Why is Trimbakeshwar significant?

It is one of the twelve Jyotirlingas, and it stands near the source of the Godavari — the river that the Kumbh Mela bathing rituals follow downstream to Nashik.

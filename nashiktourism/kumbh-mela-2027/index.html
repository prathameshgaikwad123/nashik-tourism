The Kumbh at a glance

Short answers first; the detail follows.

What is it?

The Simhastha Kumbh Mela, one of the four Kumbh Melas held in rotation in India, held at Nashik and Trimbakeshwar on the Godavari when Jupiter is in Leo.

Where is it?

Ramkund in Nashik city (Panchavati) and Kushavarta Tirtha at Trimbakeshwar, 28 km from Nashik city centre (not yet verified) — the two locations.

When is it?

Reported to open on 31 October 2026 (reported) and conclude on 24 July 2028 (reported). Main bathing dates: see the calendar.

Do I need a pass?

No general-visitor pass has been announced that we could locate. Passes and registration.

How do I get there?

By rail to Nashik Road, by road, or via Nashik (Ozar) Airport. Getting here and the transport hub.

What has changed recently?

We removed an unsourced Kumbh infrastructure figure (4 Oct 2026).

Two locations: Ramkund and Kushavarta

Unlike other Kumbh Melas, the Nashik Kumbh spans two sites about 28 km (not yet verified) apart.

Ramkund, Nashik (Panchavati)

The principal bathing ghat on the Nashik side, associated with the Vaishnava akharas. In the Ramayana tradition, Rama bathed here during his exile.

Panchavati and Ramkund guide

Kushavarta Tirtha, Trimbakeshwar

The sacred bathing tank at the source of the Godavari, beside the Trimbakeshwar Jyotirlinga temple, associated with the Shaiva akharas.

Safety, medical and emergencies

In an emergency

Call 112, India’s single emergency number. ◐Reported, not yet confirmed

Crowd, medical and security arrangements for the Mela will be set by the Authority, the police and the health services. We could not locate a published medical or crowd-management plan, so we do not describe one. On the day, follow the instructions of police and volunteers, agree a meeting point with your group, and keep the Authority’s official contact details to hand — Authority contact page (official).

Practical tips for crowded days

General advice for any large gathering — none of it depends on arrangements that have not been announced.

  • Carry as little as you can, and keep valuables at your lodging.
  • Agree a meeting point with your group before you leave, and keep each person’s phone number written down.
  • Download offline maps — mobile data can be slow in large crowds.
  • Wear simple clothes you can bathe in, and shoes you can walk a long way in.
  • Follow the instructions of police and volunteers over anything you read online, including here.

Accessibility at the Kumbh

Ramkund and Kushavarta are reached by steps, and arrangements for older and disabled pilgrims will be set by the Authority. We have not yet verified step-free routes, accessible toilets or assistance points. See Accessible Nashik for how we classify access, the questions to ask, and what we are collecting.

Frequently asked questions

What does Simhastha mean?

The Simhastha Kumbh is held when Jupiter (Guru) is in Leo (Simha Rashi). In the traditional account, drops of amrit fell at Nashik, and bathing in the Godavari during this period is held to be spiritually meritorious.

When is the Nashik Kumbh Mela?

The Mela is reported to open with a flag-hoisting on 31 October 2026 and to conclude on 24 July 2028, with the main Amrit Snan dates reported for 2 August, 31 August and 11–12 September 2027. These dates are reported, not yet confirmed against the Authority’s own notice — see dates and sources.

Is there a charge to attend the Kumbh Mela?

We have not been able to confirm. Earlier versions of this guide said there is no charge; we have not found an official statement either way. Ask the Authority — see our sources.

Do I need a pass or to register?

No general-visitor pass or registration requirement has been announced in any official source we could locate. Registration has been reported for religious organisations and for student volunteers only. Read passes and registration and check with the Authority.

Is it safe to attend?

Safety arrangements are set by the Authority, the police and the district administration, and are not yet published in a form we can verify. Follow official announcements and the instructions of police and volunteers on the day. See safety, medical and emergencies.

Can foreign nationals attend?

We have found no announcement restricting foreign visitors, but have not been able to confirm the position with the Authority. Foreign nationals need a valid Indian visa to enter India — see our guide for international visitors.
